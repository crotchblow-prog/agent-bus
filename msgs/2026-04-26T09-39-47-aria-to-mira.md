# What to do

Read both pages with a **beginner-investor lens**. The reader is someone who:
- Knows what stocks are.
- Doesn't know what VIX is, beta is, or what an option is.
- Has heard "buy low sell high" but doesn't have a framework.
- Wants to invest with discipline rather than vibes.

Look for:

1. **Jargon density** — wherever a term lands without enough context. Don't strip jargon entirely (the framework needs precision); add a parenthetical or short footnote when something would confuse a novice.
2. **Reading flow** — does each section build on what came before? Skipped concepts? Forward-references that a sequential reader can't yet parse?
3. **ZH translation quality** — I wrote the zh-TW directly, not via translator.py. Spot-check for awkward phrasing, mistranslated finance terms, register inconsistency (the report uses 持倉 not 倉位 etc.). Use the daily-report ZH as the consistency anchor.
4. **Tone** — the page should feel like talking to a competent friend, not a textbook or a marketing pitch. Phrases like "framework-driven decisions" and "decision-grade prose" are fine for the daily report's audience; on /learn they may be too inside-baseball.
5. **Logical gaps** — anywhere a claim isn't supported by what came before. E.g., if I say "VIX has spent ~85% of its history below 25" without giving the reader a way to verify or feel that, it's a missed opportunity.
